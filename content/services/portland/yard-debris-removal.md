## What we take
If it grew in the yard or blew into it, we can most likely haul it.

- Branches, limbs and brush
- Bagged leaves, grass clippings and hedge trimmings
- Blackberry canes and ivy pulled off a fence line
- Old wood fencing, lattice and loose fence wire, rolled up
- [[Small stumps already out of the ground, if two people can lift them]]
- Cracked planters, broken trellises and landscape timbers
- Patio furniture the wind threw around, a bent trampoline or a broken playset
- The Christmas tree, cut down to size, in January

[[Soil, sod, rock, gravel and concrete are too heavy for our flat load prices, so call us about those.]]

## Where it goes
Green waste is the easiest part of any load to keep out of the landfill.

- Branches, brush, leaves and clippings go to [[a yard debris composting facility]], where they are ground into mulch and compost.
- Metal fence posts, wire and furniture frames go to a scrap recycler.
- Painted or treated wood, plastic and broken pots go to a transfer station, [[Metro Central or Metro South]] in Oregon and [[a Clark County transfer station]] in Washington.

Leave the piles mixed if that is easier for you. We split them as we load.

## Free options
A small pile may not need us at all.

- If your garbage service includes a yard debris cart, fill it each week and a modest cleanup is gone in a month or so.
- Fallen leaves can stay in your flower beds as mulch through the winter.
- Split firewood and good rounds from a downed tree get claimed quickly when offered free on a Buy Nothing group.
- [[Some cities and neighborhoods hold free chipping or yard debris days in the spring]], so check with yours.

When the pile would take months of carts, or a storm dropped a whole tree's worth of limbs, one visit clears it in an afternoon.

## How the job goes
Send a photo of the pile from a few steps back so we can see all of it. [[A pile about the size of a pickup bed fits a 1/4 truck; a whole yard's spring cleanup is often a 1/2 truck, and a big storm a full one.]]

Stack the branches near the driveway if you can, cut to lengths that are easy to carry, and bag or rake the loose leaves into a heap. A pile out back is fine too: tell us when you book, and we carry it to the truck.

On the day, we text before we head over, look at the pile with you, and confirm the size before loading. A pile bigger than you booked costs more only if you agree to the new price; a smaller one gets money back. We rake up the twigs and leaves where the pile stood.

After a storm, stay clear of downed power lines and leave any tree resting on the house to an arborist. We come once the danger has passed.

## FAQ
Q: Do the leaves need to be bagged?
A: No. Bags make loading faster, but a tidy heap works too. [[We rake loose leaves onto a tarp and carry them to the truck.]]

Q: Can you take dirt, rock or sod?
A: [[No. They are too heavy for our flat prices.]] Call us with how much there is, and we tell you what we can do or who can help.

Q: How short do I need to cut the branches?
A: [[Short enough for two people to carry through your gate, about eight feet or less.]] If a trunk is too thick to cut, leave it whole and send a photo so we can plan for it.

Q: Can you come after a windstorm?
A: Yes, once the roads are open and the power lines are safe. Book the first open day on the order page, and keep away from anything touching a wire until the utility has been out.

Q: Will you take the old fence too?
A: Yes. Stack the panels, roll up loose wire and pull the nails you can reach. [[Fence posts still set in concrete are too heavy to come along.]]
