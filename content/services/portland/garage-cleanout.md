## What we take
Almost anything that ends up in a garage can ride in our truck. Most garage loads are a mix of furniture that moved out of the house, boxes from the last two moves, and the tools, toys and sports gear nobody reaches for anymore.

- Old couches, chairs, dressers, mattresses and rugs
- Bikes, scooters, wagons, kayaks and outgrown toys
- Treadmills, exercise bikes and weight benches
- Metal shelving, workbenches and cracked storage bins
- Lawn mowers and power tools, [[once the gas and oil are drained]]
- Scrap wood, old fencing and [[small amounts of leftover building material]]
- A spare fridge or chest freezer
- Moving boxes, bagged trash and broken holiday decorations

A few things stay off the truck because they need special handling: wet paint, fuel, motor oil, propane tanks, pool chemicals and pesticides. Set them aside before we arrive and we will tell you where each one can go. Paint cans that are empty and dried out are fine.

## Where it goes
We sort every garage load on the way out, and the landfill is the last stop, not the first.

- Furniture, tools, bikes and building material with life left in them go to donation, [[usually a Habitat for Humanity ReStore or a charity thrift store]].
- Metal goes to a scrap recycler: bike frames, shelving, grills and mower decks.
- Moving boxes go to cardboard recycling, flattened and dry.
- A fridge or freezer goes to an appliance recycler [[licensed to recover the refrigerant]].
- What is left goes to a transfer station to be weighed and sorted again: [[Metro Central or Metro South]] on the Oregon side, [[a Clark County transfer station]] for jobs across the river.

The disposal fees are part of your load price. There is no separate dump bill after the job.

## Free options
Clearing a garage does not always need a paid haul, and we would rather you hear about the free routes from us first.

- Set usable things at the curb with a free sign, or post them in a neighborhood Buy Nothing group. Bikes, shelving and hand tools tend to go fast.
- [[The Habitat for Humanity ReStores in the metro pick up some donated furniture and building material at no charge.]] Call ahead, since they turn down worn or broken pieces.
- Scrap metal yards take metal for free, and some pay by the pound for it.
- Leftover house paint goes back for free through [[PaintCare drop off sites, many of them paint stores, in both Oregon and Washington]].
- Household chemicals go to [[Metro's hazardous waste facilities in Oregon, or Clark County's in Washington, free for small household amounts]].

The free routes take your time and a vehicle that can carry the load. When the pile is mostly broken, heavy or worn out, that is when a haul earns its price.

## How the job goes
Send a photo of the garage, or pick the size that looks like the pile. [[A single car garage that has been filling for a few years usually fits in a 1/2 truck, and a two car garage stacked to the rafters is often a full truck.]]

Pick a day and an arrival window, and we text you when we are on the way.

When we arrive, we walk the garage with you and confirm the size before anything is lifted. If the pile is bigger than the size you booked, we show you the new price first and charge the difference only if you agree. If it is smaller, we refund the difference.

[[Two of us]] carry everything out, load the truck, and sweep the floor where the pile stood. You get before and after photos by email. You do not have to be there if the garage is open or the things are out on the driveway; just leave a note when you book.

## FAQ
Q: Do I have to sort everything before you come?
A: No. Point at what goes and what stays, and the sorting is our job. The only things to pull out first are paint, fuel and chemicals, since those cannot ride with the load.

Q: How do I know which size my garage is?
A: Send a photo and we pick the size for you. If the pile turns out bigger or smaller on the day, we show you the new price before we load anything, and a bigger load is charged only if you agree.

Q: Can you take the old fridge out of the garage too?
A: Yes. It goes in the same load and on to an appliance recycler. Empty it and unplug it the day before so it can drain.

Q: Will you take down the shelves on the wall?
A: [[Yes, if they come off with a drill or a screwdriver. We leave anything wired or plumbed in, like a garage door opener or a utility sink.]]

Q: Do I need to be home for a garage cleanout?
A: No, as long as we can get in. Leave the garage door open or set everything on the driveway, add a note when you book, and we text you when we are on the way and send photos when we are done.
