## What we take
Large or small, working or broken.

- Fridges, upright freezers and chest freezers
- Washers and dryers
- Ranges, wall ovens and cooktops
- Dishwashers and microwaves
- Water heaters, drained first
- Window air conditioners and dehumidifiers
- Wine coolers and mini fridges
- TVs, computers, monitors and printers

[[We do not disconnect gas lines, water lines or wiring. A gas range or dryer needs its gas shut off and capped by a plumber or the gas company before we come, and a dishwasher or water heater needs its lines undone.]] Once it is unplugged and unhooked, it is ready for us.

## Where it goes
Each appliance goes where its parts can be used again.

- Fridges, freezers, air conditioners, dehumidifiers and wine coolers go to [[an appliance recycler licensed to recover the refrigerant before the metal is crushed]].
- Washers, dryers, ranges, dishwashers and water heaters go to a scrap metal recycler.
- TVs and computers go to an electronics recycler, [[through Oregon E-Cycles in Oregon and E-Cycle Washington across the river]].
- A clean, working appliance that is not too old [[may go to a Habitat for Humanity ReStore]] instead.

[[What the recycler charges to recover the refrigerant is part of your load price]], so nothing extra shows up after the job.

## Free options
- [[TVs, computers, monitors and printers can go free to an Oregon E-Cycles drop off site in Oregon, or an E-Cycle Washington site in Washington]], often at a thrift store or a recycler near you.
- [[Power companies in the area have run programs that pick up a working fridge or freezer and pay a small rebate]]; a call is worth it if yours still runs.
- When you buy a new washer or fridge, ask the store to take the old one away at delivery. [[Many do, some for a fee.]]
- Scrap metal yards take washers, dryers and ranges, [[though most will not take a fridge until the refrigerant is out]].

## How the job goes
Pick Single item for one appliance. For a washer and dryer pair, or a few machines at once, send a photo and we pick the size for you.

Get it ready the day before. Unplug a fridge or freezer, empty it and let it defrost with towels on the floor. Unhook the washer hoses and let them drain into a bucket. A water heater takes an hour or more to drain through a garden hose, so start it early.

Choose a day and a two hour window, and we text as we leave for your address. On arrival we look at the appliances with you and confirm the size; anything more than you booked is charged only if you agree to the new price, and anything less comes back to you.

We bring an appliance dolly and straps, cover the floor along the way out, and bring a fridge up from the basement if that is where it lives. [[Two of us]] come for most appliance jobs.

## FAQ
Q: Can you get a fridge out of the basement?
A: Yes. We bring straps and an appliance dolly for the stairs. Tell us about tight turns when you book, and send a photo of the stairs if you can.

Q: Do I have to unhook the washer and dryer?
A: Yes, please. Unplug them, turn off the water and drain the hoses. [[A gas dryer needs its gas line shut off and capped by a plumber or the gas company first.]]

Q: Is there an extra fee for the freon?
A: [[No. Getting the refrigerant out is part of the load price.]] A fridge counts toward the size like any other item.

Q: Can you take my old TV and computer too?
A: Yes, and they go to an electronics recycler. [[If you would rather drop them off yourself, Oregon E-Cycles and E-Cycle Washington sites take them for free.]]
