## What we take
We take the whole setup, not only the shell.

- Above ground hot tubs and spas of most sizes
- The cover, the cover lift and the steps
- The wooden or composite skirt around the tub
- The pump, the heater and the control box, [[once the power is disconnected]]
- [[A small platform built only for the tub, if it unscrews]]
- Inflatable and portable spas

[[Swim spas and tubs set into the ground or into a deck are bigger jobs, so we price those by phone.]] [[The concrete pad or pavers under the tub stay where they are.]]

## Where it goes
A hot tub is several very different materials in one box, and each one goes its own way.

- The motor, the pump, the heater and any steel frame go to a scrap metal recycler.
- The acrylic or fiberglass shell, the foam insulation and the waterlogged cover go to a transfer station: [[Metro Central or Metro South]] for Oregon jobs, [[a Clark County transfer station]] for Washington.
- A cedar skirt in good shape can go to [[wood recycling]]; painted or rotted panels ride along with the shell.
- A tub that still runs and is fairly new may find a new owner, so tell us if yours works.

The fee the transfer station charges for the shell is built into the load price, so the bill does not grow after we weigh in.

## Free options
A tub that still heats and holds water can sometimes go for nothing.

- Offer it free online, with a clear note that the new owner has to move it. People who want a spa often bring their own trailer and a few strong friends.
- [[Some spa dealers take the old tub away when they deliver a new one]], so ask before you buy.
- If only the metal parts matter to you, a scrap yard takes motors and pumps, but getting them out safely means the power has to be off first.

Most tubs we haul are past saving: a cracked shell, a dead heater, a cover soaked through. That is the job a paid haul is for.

## How the job goes
Send photos of the tub, the gate and the path to the street. [[Most tubs fit a 1/2 truck, and a large one with its steps and skirt often takes a 3/4 truck.]] Narrow side yards and stairs change the plan, so we look at the route before we confirm.

Before we come, drain the tub completely and leave the cover off so the inside can dry. [[Have a licensed electrician disconnect the tub's power at the breaker panel and cap the wires, since we do not do electrical work.]]

On the day, we text when we set out, then walk the route with you and confirm the size. A bigger load than you booked is charged only after you agree to its price; a smaller one gets the difference back. [[If the tub cannot fit through the gate in one piece, we cut it into sections with a reciprocating saw right where it sits.]] [[Three of us]] carry it out, and we sweep the pad before we leave.

## FAQ
Q: Do I have to drain the hot tub before you come?
A: Yes. Start a day or two ahead with a garden hose or a small pump, and leave the cover off so the bottom dries. A tub with water still in it is too heavy to move.

Q: Who disconnects the power?
A: [[A licensed electrician, before we arrive. We cannot load a tub that is still wired in, so book the electrician first and then pick your day with us.]]

Q: Will you have to cut it up?
A: [[Only when it will not fit through the gate or around the house whole. We cut it where it sits and clean up the pieces and the dust.]]

Q: Can you take the cover and the steps too?
A: Yes. They go in the same load as the tub, so they are part of the size you book. Leave them by the tub and point them out on the day.
