## What we take
If you sit on it, sleep on it or keep things in it, it can almost certainly ride in our truck. One piece or a whole room, it is the same job for us.

- Couches, loveseats, sectionals and sleeper sofas
- Recliners and lift chairs, [[power ones once they are unplugged]]
- Bed frames, headboards, bunk beds and cribs
- Dressers, nightstands, armoires and wardrobes
- Dining tables, kitchen chairs and bar stools
- Desks, office chairs, bookcases and filing cabinets
- Entertainment centers and TV stands, the old TV included
- Patio tables, outdoor sofas and folding chairs

[[A piano, a gun safe or a pool table is a different kind of job, with more people and different equipment, so call us before you book one of those.]]

## Where it goes
Every piece gets a look before it is loaded, because a sound dresser deserves better than a landfill.

- Solid wood, clean upholstery and working office chairs go to donation, [[most often a charity thrift store or a Habitat for Humanity ReStore]].
- Metal bed frames, filing cabinets and the steel folding bed inside a sleeper sofa go to a scrap recycler.
- Torn, stained or broken pieces go to a transfer station to be weighed and sorted once more: [[Metro Central or Metro South]] for Oregon homes, [[a Clark County station]] for homes in Washington.

Donation centers are strict about upholstery. A couch with a rip, a stain or a sagging seat usually gets turned away, so we do that sorting on our side rather than asking you to guess. Drop off fees are already counted in your load price.

## Free options
Furniture in good condition can often find a new home with no truck from us at all.

- Offer it on a neighborhood Buy Nothing group or an online marketplace. Dressers, bookcases and dining sets usually find someone within a day or two.
- [[Some charities in the metro, the Habitat for Humanity ReStores among them, pick up furniture in good condition at no charge.]] Book early, because their pickup days fill up.
- Leaving a rental? Ask the landlord or the next tenant first, since a solid bed or table is sometimes welcome.

A worn couch, steep stairs or a move out date this week is where paying for the haul starts to make sense.

## How the job goes
Pick a size or send us a photo. One couch, one bed set or one dresser is a Single item. [[A bedroom set usually fits a 1/4 truck, and a full living room a 1/2 truck.]]

Leave every piece where it is, since there is no need to drag anything to the door. Choose a day and a two hour window, and a text from us lets you know the truck is coming.

When we arrive, we look over the pieces with you and confirm the size before any lifting starts. If there is more than you booked, you see the new price first and we charge it only if you agree. If there is less, the difference goes back on your card.

We take the legs off a table or the feet off a couch when that is what it takes to clear a doorway, and we guard door frames and railings on the way out. [[Two of us]] come for most furniture jobs.

## FAQ
Q: Do you take a sleeper sofa?
A: Yes. The fold out bed and its metal frame come with the couch as one piece. Tell us it is a sleeper when you book, since it weighs far more than it looks, and fold the bed in so it stays shut on the stairs.

Q: Can you take just one couch?
A: Yes. Pick Single item on the order page and you see the price with travel before you book. If more pieces go too, send a photo and we pick the size for you.

Q: Do I need to move the furniture outside first?
A: No. We carry it from the room it is in, upstairs or down. Clear a path through the hall, and tell us about tight turns when you book.

Q: Will you take apart a bed frame or a sectional?
A: [[Yes, when it comes apart with ordinary hand tools.]] Most bed frames and sectionals do, and taking them apart is easier on your walls.

Q: Can my old couch be donated?
A: Maybe. If it is clean, whole and free of pet hair and smoke, we try donation first; if not, it goes to the transfer station. Either way the sorting is ours to do.
