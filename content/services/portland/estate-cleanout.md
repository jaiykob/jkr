## What we take
Everything that remains once the family has chosen what to keep. We clear a home from top to bottom: attic, bedrooms, kitchen, basement, garage and shed.

- Furniture in every room, beds and mattresses included
- Clothing, linens, shoes and coats from every closet
- Dishes, pots, small appliances and the fridge
- Books, records, picture frames and holiday decorations
- Boxes and file cabinets, once the papers have been gone through
- Tools, garden equipment and the workbench
- Sewing machines, exercise machines and old electronics

What stays off the truck is the short list from any job: wet paint, fuel, propane, chemicals and medical sharps. [[If we come across prescription medicine, we hand it to you for a pharmacy take back bin.]]

## Where it goes
A lifetime in one house leaves many things that are still useful, so donation comes first.

- Furniture, kitchenware, clothing and books in good shape go to donation, [[to a charity thrift store or a Habitat for Humanity ReStore]].
- If the estate needs a receipt for what was donated, tell us when you book and we ask for one at the drop off.
- Metal, electronics and cardboard each go to their own recycler.
- The rest goes to a transfer station: [[Metro South in Oregon City or Metro Central]] on the Oregon side, [[a Clark County transfer station]] for Washington homes.

Nothing can come back off a load once it has left the driveway, so we only carry out what you have pointed to.

## Free options
Part of a home can leave without a paid haul, and it is worth starting there.

- Family first. Give each person a set time to walk through and tag what they want.
- An estate sale company sells the antiques, collections and good furniture and keeps a share of what sells, so nothing is paid up front. [[Many of them clear only what sells]], so plan for what remains.
- [[Charities in the metro pick up furniture and household goods in good condition at no charge.]]
- Neighbors and Buy Nothing groups are quick to take garden tools, canning jars and craft supplies.

What is worn, broken or simply more than one family can give away is where we come in.

## How the job goes
Start with photos of each room, or call and walk us through the house. [[A three bedroom home usually fills a full truck, and sometimes more.]] For more than one truck, call or text us and we plan the visits together.

Before the day, put colored tape on anything that stays and keep the important papers with you. When we arrive, we walk the house with whoever is there and confirm the size before anything moves. Should the load be bigger than you booked, we show the new price and load the extra only once you agree; should it be smaller, we refund the difference.

[[Two or three of us]] clear one room at a time, carry everything out with care for the walls and floors, and sweep each room as it empties. [[We can come the day after an estate sale ends to take what is left.]] If the family lives out of town, a lockbox or a neighbor with a key is enough, and we email before and after photos when the house is clear.

## FAQ
Q: Can you come the day after the estate sale?
A: [[Yes. Book the morning after the sale closes, and we take whatever the sale company leaves behind.]]

Q: I live out of state. Do I have to be there?
A: No. Leave a lockbox code, or have a neighbor or the realtor let us in, and add a note when you book. If the load is bigger than booked, we text you the new price and wait for your answer before loading it.

Q: What if we find something we want to keep partway through?
A: Tell the crew and we set it aside. Anything still in the house or on our truck can come back to you; once a load has left, it cannot, so tag the keepers before we start.

Q: Will you take the clothes, the dishes and everything in the drawers?
A: Yes. There is no need to empty drawers or box up the kitchen. Just pull out papers, jewelry and anything personal you want to keep.

Q: Can the estate get a receipt for donations?
A: Yes. Ask when you book, and we request a receipt at the drop off and pass it on to you.
