## What we take
Whatever you decide can go. That decision is always yours, and nothing leaves without it.

- Bags, boxes and piles of papers, mail and magazines
- Clothing, shoes and linens
- Furniture buried under other things, broken or whole
- Kitchen items, food containers and spoiled food
- Appliances that stopped working years ago
- Things stacked in hallways, the garage, the carport and the yard

[[We are a hauling crew, not a cleaning company. We do not clean biohazards such as needles, human or animal waste, mold, or anything soaked in bodily fluids. If we find any, we stop in that area, tell you, and come back after a licensed cleaner has been.]] The short list we cannot take on any job, like paint, fuel and propane, applies here too.

## Where it goes
We sort on the way out, the same as on any other job.

- Clothing, furniture and household goods in good shape go to donation, [[at a charity thrift store]].
- Metal, cardboard and electronics go to their recyclers.
- [[Papers with names, account numbers or medical details go in a box for you to keep or shred, never loose in the load.]]
- The rest goes to a transfer station, [[Metro Central or Metro South]] for Oregon homes and [[a Clark County station]] for Washington.

If you spot something you thought was lost, say so, and it stays with you.

## Free options
Clearing a home can begin without us, and some people would rather start that way.

- Fill your own garbage and recycling carts a little each week. It is slow, but it costs nothing extra.
- A trusted friend or family member can help you decide, one box at a time.
- Usable furniture and clothing can go to a charity pickup or a neighborhood Buy Nothing group.
- Metal can go to a scrap yard, which takes it free.

If there is a deadline, like a landlord's inspection, a sale or a move into care, a haul gets it done in days instead of months.

## How the job goes
It starts with a quiet conversation. Call or text, or send a few photos, and tell us what you hope to get done and what has to stay. Nobody needs to explain how the home got this way.

Most homes like this fill at least one full truck. For more than that, we plan a few visits on days that suit you, so it never has to happen all at once. [[We come in one truck with no large signs, park close to the door, and keep the work quiet.]]

On the day, we confirm the size with you before lifting, and anything beyond what you booked is charged only once you agree to its price. We work one room or one corner at a time, show you anything we are unsure about, and stop whenever you want to look through a box. [[Two or three of us]] come, and we sweep each space as it empties.

## FAQ
Q: Do I have to clean or sort before you come?
A: No. Leave everything as it is. We sort as we go, and you only tell us what stays.

Q: Will anyone else know about my house?
A: No. What we see stays with us. [[We never post photos of a job]], and the before and after photos go only to you.

Q: Can we go slowly and keep some things?
A: Yes. You set the pace and you decide what stays. We can split the work over several visits and stop any time you need a break.

Q: Can I set this up for my mom?
A: Yes. Book it in your name and tell us who will be at the home. We go at her pace and check with her before anything goes.

Q: Do you clean once everything is out?
A: [[We sweep, but we do not deep clean or handle biohazards.]] If the home needs that, we help you time the cleaner's visit so the two jobs fit together.
