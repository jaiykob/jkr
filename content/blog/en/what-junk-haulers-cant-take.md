Almost everything in a garage or a basement can go on our truck. A short list cannot, and it is the same list for every honest hauler, because these materials need special handling that a regular load does not get. Knowing the list ahead of time saves you an awkward moment on the day.

## The list

- **Paint, stains and solvents**, unless the can is empty and dried out.
- **Chemicals**: pool chemicals, pesticides, weed killer, drain cleaner, automotive fluids.
- **Fuel and propane**: gas cans with fuel in them, propane tanks of any size, lighter fluid.
- **Asbestos**: old pipe wrap, some floor tiles and popcorn ceilings in older homes.
- **Medical waste**: needles, sharps and anything that has been in contact with them.

## Why these stay off the truck

A load of furniture and boxes gets sorted for donation, recycling and the transfer station. None of those places can take a half full can of thinner or a propane cylinder mixed in with a couch. A leaking can soaks everything around it, and a pressurized tank can rupture in a compactor. Asbestos needs licensed abatement, not a hand truck.

If we find one of these in a load, we set it aside, leave it with you, and tell you where it can go. It does not count toward your load size.

## Where they can go instead

In the Oregon part of the metro, [[Metro runs household hazardous waste facilities at its Central and South transfer stations, free for small household amounts]]. On the Washington side, [[Clark County takes household hazardous waste at its transfer stations on set days]]. Check the hours before you load the car, because these facilities keep shorter schedules than the regular drop off.

For paint, ask the paint store about take back: [[Oregon and Washington both run paint recycling programs through participating stores]]. Propane tanks can often be exchanged where you buy them.

## The gray areas

- **Empty paint cans.** If the paint is dry and solid, the can goes with your load like any other metal.
- **Car batteries and tires.** [[These can usually come with a load for an added fee]], because the recycler charges us to take them.
- **Electronics.** TVs, computers and monitors are fine. They go to an electronics recycler, not the landfill.
- **Appliances with refrigerant.** Fridges and freezers are welcome; they go to a recycler licensed to remove the refrigerant.

## Before you book

Walk the space once and pull out anything on the list. If you are not sure about something, snap a photo of it with the rest of the pile on our [quote page](/book/quote.html), or ask us in the [FAQ](/faq.html) or by text. We would rather answer a question than turn you down at the curb. Whether you are in [Milwaukie](/milwaukie.html) or [Vancouver](/vancouver.html), the rules for these materials are about the same, and so is our answer.
