An old piano is often the heaviest thing in a house, and it was built to stay where it was put. Most junk is a question of how much room it takes in the truck. A piano is a question of weight, balance and the way out of the house, so it gets a plan of its own.

## Why a piano is different

An upright piano weighs [[300 to 800 pounds]], and a grand [[can weigh well over that]]. Most of that weight sits in a cast iron plate inside the case, high up and off to one side, so a piano tips more easily than its size suggests. Its small legs and casters were never meant to roll across a lawn or bump over a threshold.

That is why we never move a piano like a dresser. It rides on [[a padded piano board with straps]], we cover the floors and door frames on the way out, and we walk the route before anyone lifts. [[We move uprights and spinets ourselves; for a grand piano we may send you to a piano mover.]]

## Stairs change the job

A piano on the ground floor with a straight path to the driveway is a short job. Add a few porch steps, a split level entry or a basement, and it becomes a slow and careful one, because every step means lifting the full weight while keeping it level. When you get in touch, tell us how many steps there are inside and outside, and whether there is a tight turn on the way. A photo of the piano and a photo of the stairs tell us most of what we need.

## The crew it takes

Most of our jobs need [[two people]]. A piano needs a crew of [[three]]: two to carry, and one to guide, watch the corners and hold the doors. That is the main reason we ask you to call or text before you book a piano, so the right people come on the right day.

## Donation first

A piano that still plays well can have a second life. Before it goes on the truck, ask a school, a church, a community music program or a piano shop nearby whether they want it. [[Many will only take a piano that was tuned recently and has no cracked soundboard]], so ask before you promise it to anyone. If nobody wants it, [[most of a piano can be recycled]]: the iron plate, the strings and much of the wood. Anything we haul goes to donation when it can, to recycling when it cannot, and only what is left goes to the transfer station.

## What you can do before the day

- Clear the route from the piano to the door, rugs and pictures near the corners included.
- Empty the bench and the top of the piano.
- If you know the make or roughly how old it is, tell us, since it helps with donation.
- Keep children and pets out of the way while the piano is moving.
