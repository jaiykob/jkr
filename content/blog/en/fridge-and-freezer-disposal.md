Refrigerators, freezers, wine coolers and window air conditioners all have one thing in common: a sealed system full of refrigerant. That gas cannot just be vented, so these appliances go to a recycler that is licensed to recover it before the metal is crushed. That is the main reason you cannot simply leave an old fridge at the curb.

## Get it ready the week before

- **Empty it completely.** Food left inside turns into a mess in a day or two once the power is off.
- **Defrost it.** Unplug it at least a day ahead, prop the doors open and lay towels down. A freezer full of ice is heavy and leaks across your floor on the way out.
- **Tape the doors shut** on the day, so they do not swing open on the stairs.
- **Shut off the water line** if it has an ice maker, and disconnect it.

## Why the doors matter

An old fridge left in a yard or a garage with its doors on is a real hazard for children, who can climb in and get trapped. If yours has to sit outside for even a day, take the doors off or tape them shut.

## Your options

Some power companies [[run appliance recycling programs that pick up a working fridge or freezer and pay you a small rebate]]. It is worth a call if yours still runs and you are not in a hurry.

## Washers, dryers and water heaters

These have no refrigerant, so they are simpler. A washer or dryer counts as its own item in the quote. A water heater should be drained before we come, which can take an hour or more with a garden hose, so start early.
