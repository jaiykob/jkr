People often ask us, a little guiltily, where their old things end up. It is a fair question. Most of a typical load does not go to the landfill at all, and the order we sort it in is the reason.

## First stop: donation

Anything with life left in it goes to donation before anything else. That means solid furniture, working lamps, kitchenware, tools, bikes, books and clean linens. [[We drop donations at charity thrift stores in the metro on our way to recycling]], and if you want a receipt for your taxes, tell us when you book and we will ask for one.

What donation centers will not take is just as important: stained or broken furniture, used mattresses, and anything with a missing part. Those keep going down the line.

## Second stop: recycling

A surprising share of a load is recyclable once it is sorted:

- **Metal**: bed frames, shelving, grills, filing cabinets, broken appliances.
- **Electronics**: TVs, computers, monitors and printers, which go to an electronics recycler.
- **Cardboard**: moving boxes, flattened and kept dry.
- **Appliances**: fridges and freezers go to a recycler licensed to recover the refrigerant.
- **Yard debris**: branches and brush go to compost, not the landfill.

## Last stop: the transfer station

What is left goes to a transfer station, where loads are weighed and sorted again before the rest is shipped to a landfill. In the Oregon part of the metro that usually means [[Metro Central in northwest Portland or Metro South in Oregon City]]; on the Washington side, [[a Clark County transfer station in Vancouver]]. We pay the fee per load, and it is already part of your price.

## What this means for you

You do not need to sort anything. Pile it all together and point; the sorting is our job. The only things to pull out first are the items on the [can't take list](/blog/what-junk-haulers-cant-take.html), like paint and propane.

If something in the pile is worth selling, set it aside before we come. We are glad to take it, but a dresser that could fetch a fair price online should not ride to the thrift store by accident.

## A load from start to finish

Say a family in [Oregon City](/oregon-city.html) clears a basement: an old sectional, two bikes, a broken freezer, boxes of books and some scrap lumber. The bikes and books go to donation, the freezer to the appliance recycler, the boxes to cardboard recycling, the sectional and the lumber to the transfer station.
