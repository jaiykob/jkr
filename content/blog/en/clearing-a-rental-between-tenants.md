Every landlord knows the moment: the tenant is gone, the keys are back, and the unit still holds a couch, a mattress and a pile of bags. Every day it sits there is a day it cannot be shown. Here is how to turn it quickly.

## Document before anything moves

Walk the unit with your phone and take photos and video of every room, with the date. If anything left behind might be the tenant's property, [[follow Oregon or Washington's rules for abandoned property, which can require written notice and a waiting period]] before it is removed. Your attorney or landlord association can tell you the exact steps for your situation.

## Separate what stays with the unit

Mark appliances and fixtures that belong to the property so nobody hauls the range by mistake. Pull out any papers, keys or valuables and keep them with your records.

## Book the haul around the cleaners

The best order is: haul first, then cleaners, then repairs and paint. A clear unit cleans in half the time. Book the haul for the morning after the inspection, and send photos with your quote so the load size is right on the day.

## What usually comes out of a unit

Couches and mattresses, broken furniture, kitchen leftovers, bags of clothes, old TVs, and the occasional exercise bike. Apartment complexes in places like [Wood Village](/wood-village.html) and [Clackamas](/clackamas.html) see turnovers year round, while single family rentals often need a garage or yard cleared too.
