East winds through the Gorge and the occasional ice storm leave the same mess behind: branches across the lawn, a fence panel on its side, and a yard debris cart that is full by the first afternoon. Here is how to work through it.

## Safety first

Stay away from downed power lines and anything touching them; call the utility. Do not climb a ladder in wind, and leave large trees on or near the house to an arborist. Hauling comes after the danger is gone.

## The yard debris cart

The weekly yard debris cart is the cheapest option for small storms. [[Most garbage haulers in the metro limit what goes in the cart and may take extra bundles for a fee]]. Branches usually need to be cut short to fit, and the cart goes out once a week, so a big storm can take a month of pickups to clear.

## Hauling it yourself

If you have a pickup and a free day, a yard debris drop off site will take branches and brush for a fee per load. [[Many transfer stations and landscape suppliers in the metro accept yard debris for composting]]. Bring gloves and a tarp; branches shift on the highway.

## A single haul

When the pile is bigger than a few carts, one visit is usually faster and cheaper than weeks of carts. We take branches, brush, broken fencing, wind blown patio furniture and the cracked trampoline in one trip. Each pile about the size of a pickup bed counts as a yard debris pile on our [quote page](/book/quote.html).

Cut branches to a length two people can carry, and stack them in one place near the driveway. Roll loose fencing wire so it does not snag. What we cannot take is soil, sod and rock, which are too heavy for flat pricing. Our [yard debris removal page](/portland/yard-debris-removal.html) has the full list and the sizes most cleanups fill.

## Where storm debris goes

Branches and brush go to composting, not the landfill. Metal fencing and furniture frames go to recycling. Treated wood and broken plastic go to the transfer station.

## Places that see the most

Homes near the Gorge, in [Corbett](/corbett.html) and [Troutdale](/troutdale.html), take the brunt of east wind storms, and the wooded lots up in [Sandy](/sandy.html) see plenty of fallen limbs. After a big storm the roads can stay blocked for a day or two, so we book the first clear day and work through requests in order.
