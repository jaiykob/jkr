Clearing out the home of someone you love is one of the hardest jobs a family takes on. There are decisions in every drawer, and the timeline is often set by a sale or a lease. This is a practical order that helps, written with care.

## Start with the papers, not the furniture

Before anything leaves, go through desks, file boxes and the kitchen drawer where every household keeps important things. Look for wills, deeds, titles, insurance policies, tax records, account statements and keys. Put them in one box that stays with you. Once a load leaves, nothing can be pulled back out.

## Let the family choose first

Give each person a set time to walk through and mark what they want, with colored tape or sticky notes. Photos and letters often matter more than anything valuable. Ship or deliver those pieces before the clearing starts.

## Decide what is worth selling

If the home has antiques, collections or good furniture, an estate sale company can sell them and take a share. [[Many estate sale companies in the metro clear only what sells]], so plan for what is left. We can come the day after a sale ends and take everything that remains.

## Work room by room

Clear one room completely before starting the next. It is slower in the first hour and much faster by the end of the day, and it gives the family a finished space to rest in. Leave the kitchen for last if you need somewhere to sit and eat.

## What we handle

We carry everything out, from the attic to the basement, including furniture that has not moved in decades. Usable things go to donation, metals and electronics to recycling, and the rest to the transfer station. Mark anything that stays with tape, and we leave it exactly where it is.

## Give yourself time

There is no prize for finishing in a weekend. If the timeline allows, take a break between the papers, the family walk through and the clearing. The house will still be there, and so will the help.
