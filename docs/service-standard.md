# How we treat every customer

One page for the founder and anyone who works a job. It is how Disneyland
trains its park staff (founder, 2026-10-11: "check disney land customer
service ... tailor tone and taste to junk removal"), put in plain junk removal
words. The research and its sources:
`docs/superpowers/research/2026-10-11-disneyland-practices.md`.

The homepage makes four promises (your price first, a text before we arrive,
we do all the lifting, we leave it clean). This page is how we keep them.

## 1. Our four priorities, in order

Safe, kind, tidy, on time.

When two pull against each other, the earlier one wins. We never rush a lift
to keep a window, and we never skip the sweep to make the next job.

- **Safe**: lift with two people when it is heavy, carry rather than drag,
  and stop if a stair, a floor or a load looks wrong.
- **Kind**: greet people by name, listen, and speak plainly.
- **Tidy**: what the customer sees is clean, from the truck to the driveway.
- **On time**: inside the window we promised, with a text before we arrive.

## 2. Before the job

- The confirmation email and the reminder go out by themselves.
- When we leave for the job, tap **On my way** on the dashboard (`/jkr/`). It
  opens a text to the customer. Give a time we will beat: if the drive says
  20 minutes, say about 30.
- When unsure, say the later time. Early to a time we gave feels good; late
  to it does not.
- Never give a time outside the window they booked. If we will miss it, text
  before the window starts, not after.

## 3. At the door

- Greet them by name and say who we are.
- Walk the items together before lifting anything, and confirm the size.
- If the load is bigger than they booked, show the new price on the
  dashboard first. We charge the difference only if they agree (the rule
  they agreed to when booking). If it is smaller, tell them the refund.
- Ask about anything fragile and about the way out.

## 4. On the property

- What the customer sees is always tidy: the truck, our clothes, the
  driveway, the street.
- No smoking, music or phone calls on the property.
- Park legally and never block a neighbor.
- Carry, never drag. Mind doorframes, floors and the lawn on the way out.

## 5. Answer the question behind the question

People ask one thing and often mean another. Answer both.

- "Do I need to be home?" usually means "can I book while I am at work?".
  Answer: no, if the items are outside or somewhere we can reach; leave a
  note in the booking and we text when we are on the way.
- "What if I guess the size wrong?" usually means "will I get a surprise
  bill?". Answer: no surprise. We show the new price before we lift, and we
  charge it only if you agree. A smaller load is refunded.

The FAQ answers start with the plain answer, then what to do next.

## 6. Small extras

If it fits in the size they booked, take it without making them ask twice:
the extra box in the garage, the bag by the door. Break down the box, carry
it from wherever it sits. Nothing is added while it fits.

## 7. The end is what people remember

- Walk through the space with them.
- Sweep where the junk was.
- Take the after photo. The dashboard keeps the before and after, and the
  "all done" email sends both.
- Thank them by name and ask if there is anything else before we go.

## 8. When something goes wrong

It may not be our fault, but it is our problem. Five steps, in this order
(the order Disney teaches as HEARD, in our words):

1. **Listen** to all of it. Do not interrupt or explain yet.
2. **Say we understand**: "I would be frustrated too."
3. **Say sorry** for the trouble, even when we did not cause it.
4. **Fix it now.** The crew may give back up to $25 on the spot for a small
   miss, such as a scuff or arriving past the window. Anything bigger goes to
   the founder the same day.
5. **Find why** and write it in the job's notes, so it does not happen again.
   The founder reads these every week.

For a written complaint (an email, a text, a review), the complaint reply

## 9. Spanish

Every page on the site has a Spanish version. When a customer writes or
speaks Spanish, answer in Spanish when we can (plain, with tú), and hand it to
the founder when we cannot.
